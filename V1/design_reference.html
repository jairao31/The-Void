Screen 1 — The problem
9:41●●●
The problem
You haven't been truly bored in years.
Every idle moment — a queue, a lift, a pause — you reach for your phone. Not because there's anything there. Stillness just feels wrong now.
"The average person touches their phone 2,617 times a day. Boredom never gets a chance."
That's uncomfortably true →
Screen 2 — The science
9:41●●●
The science
Boredom is where your best thinking happens.
Neuroscientists call it the Default Mode Network — the brain state active when you're doing nothing. That's where creativity, self-knowledge, and clarity live. Smartphones switched it off.
"Einstein, Darwin, Newton — all famously bored men with nowhere to be and nothing to do."
So what does this app do? →
Screen 3 — The app
9:41●●●
The Void
The Void does nothing. That's the whole point.
Set a timer. The screen goes blank. No content, no music, no guidance — just you and whatever your mind does with the silence. Afterwards log what surfaced. Over time, your stillness tolerance grows.
"Not meditation. Not mindfulness. The Void is the old, forgotten art of having nothing to do."
I want to try this →
